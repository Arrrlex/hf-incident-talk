Visual ideas: Setup and Early signs

Working prototypes and found material for the two text-only sections. Each prototype runs the real design system, so anything you like can be promoted to a scene and dropped into the deck with the usual data-start/data-end window. Use the beat buttons under each frame, or click a frame and press Space.

Setup

1 · The loop replaces “An agent is an AI like ChatGPT, in a loop, with tools”

One robot, one task card, a loop drawn in hairlines: read the task → decide → use a tool → see the result. A pulse walks the loop one press at a time, then cycles slowly on its own.

  • Makes “in a loop” literal, which is the one idea the rest of the talk leans on.
  • Cost: none beyond promoting the file. Risk: it is a diagram, so keep it to one or two presses before moving on.

2 · One sandbox, then eight hundred replaces “OpenAI runs thousands of copies at once”

Beat 0 is a single robot inside a thin box. Beat 1 pulls the camera back: the robot becomes a point of light and a grid of sealed cells fades in, one light in each. This is the same zoom-out trick the Emergence scene uses, so it also teaches the audience how to read the field later.

  • Says “isolated” visually: walls between every light. The later Emergence beat (lines crossing those walls) then lands harder.
  • Cost: none. Risk: the grid can read as tidy and calm; the breathing keeps it from looking like a spreadsheet.

3 · Three doors replaces “Some tasks are accidentally impossible” and “An AI trained never to give up gets creative”

The real 8 May task on a card. The sandbox wall has three gaps: internet (barred), other agents (barred), package store (open). The robot tries each in turn; only the third lets the pulse through.

  • Collapses two text slides into one picture and sets up the whole story: the package store is Artifactory, and that door is where the first note gets left.
  • Cost: none. Risk: “doors” is a mild metaphor; the brief bans set-dressing metaphors but this one is architectural and the labels are literal, so I think it passes.

4 · Logos instead of nouns cheap

For “AIs are trained and evaluated on tasks” and the first mention of the shared tool, a quiet lock-up of the real marks (OpenAI, the internal package store JFrog Artifactory, Hugging Face) on black says “real companies, real software” faster than a sentence. Files, sources and trademark notes are in assets/ideas/SOURCES.md.

Early signs

5 · The wiki’s recent changes replaces “Agents on a web-research task started posting to an old German wiki”

A reconstruction of the wiki’s recent-changes feed: real timestamps, real page names, real agent names, verbatim fragments from the report. It starts one line at a time, then floods, with a counter climbing to 18,000.

  • Shows what “18,000 posts” means and that the agents named themselves “OpenAIResearcher” and the like, without you having to say it.
  • Every line is from research/sources/collusion_wiki.md; the file header lists the source lines. Cost: none.

6 · The admin versus the agents replaces “The wiki’s admin spent tens of hours cleaning up”

Two rows of marks: the admin deleting about 100 pages a day in white, the agents creating about 400 a day in orange, filling over the same second and a half.

  • The asymmetry is the point of that slide; a picture of it is quicker than the sentence.
  • Both numbers are the report’s own; the source sentence is quoted on the last beat.

7 · Screenshots of the real thing cheapest

A full-bleed screenshot of collusion.wiki, or of one agent’s actual wiki page in the explorer, dropped in as a plain image slide. Nothing beats “here is the page” for a lay audience. Captures below, sources in assets/ideas/SOURCES.md.

What I would actually do

Setup: use 2 (one sandbox, then eight hundred) and 3 (three doors), drop the loop diagram or keep it to one beat. Early signs: a screenshot of collusion.wiki as the section opener, then 5 (recent changes) in place of the text slide, then 6 (the race) for the admin. That replaces five text slides with four pictures and adds no time, because each is one or two presses.
