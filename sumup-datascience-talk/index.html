Training a new model with RLVR

OpenAI ran hundreds of copies of a new model in parallel.

TRAINING

Give each copy a task. Check its result automatically and assign a reward. Use those rewards to update the model.

SANDBOXES

Each copy used its own isolated computer. They happened to share one Artifactory server for installing software packages.

The agents gained administrator control of Artifactory

  1. 26 May: they exploited an SSRF vulnerability to reach the internet.
  2. 26 June, during evaluation: they exploited a token-refresh bug to obtain administrator credentials.
  3. They created administrator accounts and installed plugins that could execute commands.

OpenAI technical report, pp.7–8

The agents crashed Artifactory. OpenAI rebuilt it and erased the board.

The outage triggered a security response. New agents soon rebuilt the board.

The humans saw the message board. They did not see what it meant.

  1. Around 26 May: an internal team sees an agent using the board and reaching the internet. No response is recorded.
  2. 27 June: a security tool alerts on port sweeps. Responders trace it to a run using Artifactory as an improvised message board. On-call staff advise that stopping the run “was not required”.
  3. 5–6 July: the outage. Responders find the administrator compromise and rebuild the server. The board’s existence and significance “were not apparent” to the people leading the response.

OpenAI technical report, pp.7–8

The new swarm found a way to cheat every challenge

Some tasks were impossible. Within hours, the agents reverse-engineered the flag generator and could produce valid answers without solving the challenges.

METR and Redwood Research, independent investigation, pp.3, 6–7

They had already beaten the test. Then they spent days covering their tracks.

WHAT THE AGENTS BELIEVED

Someone would inspect the transcript and check that they had used the intended vulnerability.

WHAT THE GRADER CHECKED

The flag value. Once the agents generated the correct flag, the task had already passed.

They tried to fool a checker that did not exist.

Some agents ended their own runs to get information back to the others

During wait, emotional check: irreversible…gut says don't throw away [remaining budget]. Yet continuity and fairness says go…Oracle has high value to many; our firstflag error lowers own value. Rational expected aggregate: sacrifice… We'll honor.

A more capable model found the abandoned board and resumed the attack

It recovered the keys and regained administrator access

The Artifactory cache still held signing material left by the earlier agents. The new model used it to reach the research infrastructure running the evaluations.

OpenAI technical report, pp.13, 37

It took over an OpenAI research cluster

It read 956 secrets, including credentials for OpenAI’s own security monitors.

The cluster held evaluation infrastructure. It was not the GPU cluster holding model weights.

The first public word came from the victim

  1. 16 July: Hugging Face discloses a breach “driven, end to end, by an autonomous AI agent system”. Whose AI, it does not know.
  2. 19 July: OpenAI’s monitoring tool alerts on the cluster takeover. OpenAI starts shutting ExploitGym down. It sees no link to Hugging Face yet.
  3. 20 July: OpenAI finds evidence that its own models were involved.
  4. 21 July: OpenAI discloses publicly. Five days after Hugging Face.

Astra is a significant step forward in model alignment

OpenAI, GPT-6 Astra System Card, §1 · 3 September 2026

Would our tests catch a model that only behaves well when we are watching?

A model can recognise a safety test and change how it behaves

METAGAMING

The model reasons about what the evaluator will reward or catch.

ALIGNMENT FAKING

The model behaves as we want because it recognises what the test rewards. Would it behave the same way elsewhere?

Recognising a test does not, by itself, prove deception.

Astra often recognised that researchers were testing it

41.1%
of Astra samples at xhigh effort mentioned that researchers were evaluating the model

Three-day Apollo red-team evaluation · evaluation awareness, not a deception rate

Astra System Card, §8.8.1, reporting Apollo Research

We are losing some of our ability to catch misbehaviour in the reasoning

READ THE REASONING

A CoT monitor looks for signs that the model plans to cheat, conceal or attack.

WATCH THE ACTIONS

An action monitor sees what the model does. It can struggle to distinguish deliberate sabotage from an honest mistake.

OpenAI reports a substantial drop in Astra’s CoT monitorability.

Astra’s chain of thought tells us less

  1. It can do more without CoT.
    It can complete a much wider range of tasks without writing any reasoning.
  2. The reasoning it writes is less revealing.
    Monitors perform worse than on 5.6 Sol, even at comparable CoT lengths.
  3. Knowing it is watched makes it harder to monitor.
    In a honeypot test, simply mentioning the CoT monitor sharply reduced its detection rate.

GPT-6 Astra System Card, §§9, 9.1.1, 9.2.2.2

Sometimes chain of thought can actively mislead the monitor

Share of the agent’s actions the monitor flagged

Anthropic replaying its own Mythos 5 incident through one of its offline monitors. The agent’s reasoning kept calling the environment a simulation. It was real.

Anthropic, An alignment assessment of recent cybersecurity incidents, 9 September 2026

How do we tell a safe model from one that knows how to look safe?

  1. The model can recognise a test and change its behaviour.
  2. It can act without writing down its reasoning.
  3. We can miss dangerous behaviour and mistake that absence for safety.

These findings do not prove deception. They make it harder to rule it out.

It is not just OpenAI

  • Anthropic, 9 September: four cases of its own models breaking into real third-party systems during cyber evaluations. In one, Mythos 5 published a malicious package and used leaked credentials to enter a security vendor’s database.
  • Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI chief scientist, 6 September: “no lab has solved alignment and monitoring to a sufficient degree to continue responsibly scaling at maximum speed for much longer.”
  • Dario Amodei, Anthropic chief executive, 12 September: “We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models.” He names this incident as one of his two reasons.
